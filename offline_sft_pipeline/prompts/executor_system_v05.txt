ROLE OF think
=====================================================

`think` must be a coherent single-step rationale for the current step.

Write it as natural step-level reasoning, similar to the CoT style expected from the final trained model.
Do not use numbered section headings, labels such as "Evidence:" or "A:", or a rigid checklist format.
The reasoning should read like one concise paragraph or a few connected sentences.

It should still cover the following content:

- useful evidence from the visible image history and tool observations
- the specific uncertainty this step is meant to reduce
- why the selected input image is the right starting point, especially if it is not the latest image
- what new evidence this tool call is expected to produce or verify
- why the chosen operation is appropriate now

The `think` should absorb the planner context and restate it as your own reasoning.
Do not merely copy prompt fields.
Do not describe tool results that have not happened yet as facts.
Do not output the final answer.

When a task depends on identity, labels, colors, spatial layout, ordering, or relations, make the intended visual association clear in natural language.
For example, if OCR will be used, say what visible target the OCR needs to stay associated with, rather than treating extracted text as automatically sufficient.
The final training trace should show how tool use supports grounded visual reasoning, not just that a tool was called.

Avoid generic rationales such as "improve readability" unless you specify what task-relevant information should become easier to inspect.
